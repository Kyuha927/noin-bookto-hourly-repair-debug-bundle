## 4. Modeling development strategy optimized for Lin

Work from large identity decisions to small detail. Do not polish buckles on a wrong face.

### Stage A: reference packet and proportion board

- Assemble an immutable private reference packet from the approved face, palette, Eighth Tag sheet, and explicit user corrections.
- Record asset IDs, hashes, crops, approval scope, and conflicts.
- Create front/side construction overlays only as private modeling guides. A generated side view is provisional and cannot override the selected front face.
- Define adult proportions, head size, shoulder/hip balance, coat length, mantle mass, boot volume, and tag/thermos scale.

### Stage B: face and head identity

- Sculpt/model the head in neutral clay first.
- Prioritize forehead/temple, cheek plane, jaw/chin, nose bridge/tip, eyelid depth, mouth support, and ear placement.
- Match the quiet R4 impression in front and three-quarter views. Avoid oversized circular anime eyes, doll stare, tiny unsupported nose, pinched chin, or smooth mask face.
- Test neutral, focused, mild irritation, small warm smile, and memory-wear restraint without changing identity.
- Use Astra visual review at each meaningful head revision, not for every vertex operation.

### Stage C: hair architecture

- Build the short cacao bob as layered masses with deliberate roots, overlaps, exposed-ear asymmetry, and readable side/back silhouette.
- Hairpin attachment must be physical and not float.
- Avoid spaghetti strands, repeated leaves, helmet shell, or generic long-hair conversion.
- Validate head turns, collar/ear clearance, and 64px silhouette.

### Stage D: body, garments, and props

- Maintain a mature, field-capable, elegant adult body with practical stance.
- Build real garment thickness, inner/outer layers, seams, closures, weight and wear.
- Use one dominant asymmetric mantle mass, not random asymmetry everywhere.
- Reduce strap repetition. Hardware follows load and attachment logic.
- Model seven engraved tags plus one unmistakably blank tag; keep them individually readable without becoming a noisy brass curtain.
- Thermos, note, pouch, scarf, hairpin, gloves, boots, and tag ring use distinct material families and plausible attachment.
- Preserve ordinary-life warmth alongside command authority.

### Stage E: topology, UV, and materials

- Separate high-detail authoring geometry from a clean deformable game-ready candidate.
- Retopologize face, shoulders, elbows, hands, hips, knees, hair deformation zones, mantle, and coat openings deliberately.
- Maintain topology identity receipts before transferring UVs, shape keys, or weights.
- Use a practical texture strategy: face/eye detail, hair direction, cloth weave/wear, leather, brass oxidation, tag engraving, and controlled dirt. Do not bake the entire 2D concept sheet onto the model.
- Use restrained anime-real materials compatible with the actual Blender renderer and the current Unity/URP path where required.
- Skin remains soft and clean, not waxy. Brass is oxidized/worn, not mirror gold. Cloth and leather must not share one plastic response.

### Stage F: expression, rig, and motion language

- Rig neutral body, face, fingers, bob, mantle, tags, scarf, and thermos attachments.
- Test blink, gaze, small smile, mild annoyance, focused command, breath, and restrained memory-wear expression.
- Preserve adult facial volume during expression interpolation.
- Build the finger-fold count gesture for 1–8 and the listening/attentive gesture.
- Eight-ring VFX or guide controls must remain separate from geometry and not cover the face.
- Test coat/mantle, tag cluster, scarf, hair and props in head turn, arm raise, walking, command pose, and seated/off-duty pose.

### Stage G: presentation and engine proof

- Keep a neutral clay presentation and a shaded presentation.
- Render front/three-quarter/side/back plus a short turntable using fixed recorded conditions.
- Use a lens and framing that do not distort the face or hide silhouette problems.
- Save and reopen in Blender. Export only formats genuinely supported by the current project, then reimport and compare scale, materials, bones and blendshapes.
- Do not claim Warudo, VRM, Unity, or runtime parity unless that exact path was executed and inspected.
